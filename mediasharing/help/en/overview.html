How Media Sharing GUI works

It makes a library on a Mac somewhere else look like it's right here on your network.

Home Sharing (Media Sharing) lets the Apple TV app, Music and iTunes stream from another computer — but only one that's on the same local network. Media Sharing GUI removes that limit. Point it at a Mac at home (or anywhere you can reach by SSH) and the library it shares appears in your Apple TV or Music source list here, as if that Mac were in the next room.

What it does, step by step

  1. Opens an SSH tunnel from this Mac to the remote one.
  2. Asks the remote Mac, over that tunnel, which Home Sharing service it is broadcasting on its own local network.
  3. Re-publishes that same service here, over Bonjour, pointed back through the tunnel — so apps on this Mac see the library as if it were nearby on the LAN.

While it runs, streaming from that library travels encrypted inside the SSH tunnel. Turn it on and off with Start and Stop in the Status box.

What you need

  • On the Mac at home: Home Sharing (or Media Sharing) turned on in the Apple TV or Music app, and Remote Login (SSH) enabled. See On the Mac at home.
  • A way to reach it: its address or dynamic-DNS name, and the SSH port your router forwards to it.
  • On this Mac: nothing special — Media Sharing GUI drives the built-in ssh and Bonjour tools for you.

Getting started

  1. Set up the Mac at home once.
  2. Fill in a connection and press Start.
  3. Choose which libraries to publish here.
